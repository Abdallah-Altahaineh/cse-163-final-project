# cse-163-final-project

Hearing the Dip - we detect many exoplanets by watching for tiny dips in a star’s brightness as a planet passes in front of it. These dips are normally read from visual graphs, which shuts out blind and low-vision people.

Our project uses sonification to turn that brightness data into sound. A star’s light is mapped to pitch, so a steady star produces a steady tone, and a transiting planet causes a brief, audible drop in pitch before the tone returns to normal. By listening, a person can identify transits, judge how long they last, and notice whether they repeat.

The goal is to make exoplanet research accessible through hearing, so blind and low-vision people can analyze real data and take part in discovery directly.
